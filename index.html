The deadlines start before the due date

You found out you’re pregnant. Between the first appointment and the due date sits a quieter job: paperwork with hard dates attached — and some of those dates arrive before you’ve told anyone at work.

The rules live in a dozen places — an Ontario employment standards guide, a Service Canada portal, a city child care site, a midwifery association page. None of them know your due date, and none of them talk to each other.

Miss one and it costs real things — a midwifery space, a place in a child care line, weeks of leave you could have planned better. It’s not that the information is secret. It’s that nobody has stitched it to your calendar.

Here’s what lands on your plan — dated to you, sourced to the official page.

  1. Contact a midwifery practice group. The official advice is to call as early in pregnancy as possible, so your midwife has space for you. (ontario.ca — Midwifery in Ontario, updated January 22, 2026)

  2. Join child care waitlists in your city — your plan dates this from your due date. In Toronto, the city-run TELCCS registry covers city centres, while private and non-profit centres take applications directly. (City of Toronto — TELCCS registration, September 23, 2025)

  3. Give your employer written notice to start pregnancy or parental leave. Ontario’s Employment Standards Act requires at least 2 weeks’ written notice before your leave begins. (Ontario Ministry of Labour — ESA guide, updated July 8, 2024)

  4. Apply for EI maternity and parental benefits. Service Canada says apply as soon as possible after you stop working — if you apply more than 4 weeks after your last day of work, you may lose benefits. (canada.ca, as of September 26, 2026)

  5. Choose standard or extended parental benefits. They pay over different lengths, and you can’t switch between them once a week of parental benefits has been paid. (canada.ca, as of September 26, 2026)

  6. Register for prenatal classes. Public health programs and private educators each open registration on their own schedule — your plan puts it on your calendar. (city public-health program pages, e.g. Toronto Public Health)

  7. Register the birth with Ontario’s 5-in-1 newborn bundle — birth registration, birth certificate, SIN, Canada Child Benefit and an RESP referral in one online application, free within the first 12 months. (ontario.ca — Register a birth, updated January 14, 2026)

  8. Sort the Canada Child Benefit. In Ontario you can usually apply for the CCB at the same time as you register the birth, at the hospital or birthing centre — and a claim can cover a period that started up to 11 months ago. (canada.ca, as of September 26, 2026)

  9. Start parental leave on time. Under Ontario’s ESA, parental leave must begin no later than 78 weeks after the birth or adoption placement. (Ontario Ministry of Labour — ESA guide, updated July 8, 2024)

  10. Change your leave plans the right way. A different notice applies to changing your leave end date, returning early, or resigning: 4 weeks’ written notice. (Ontario Ministry of Labour — ESA guide, updated July 8, 2024)

Privacy details

Who is responsible

This site is run by Canvas Tech Solutions, which is responsible for the data described here.

What we collect if you allow analytics

  • Campaign tags from the page address, when present: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_id, utm_content and utm_term. Other tags and ad-click IDs (like gclid or fbclid) are ignored.
  • The page address in a cleaned form: the site, the page path and the accepted campaign tags — not the full address with every parameter.
  • Basic browser/device details: device type, browser family and major version, operating-system family, a screen-size range, and your browser's main language. We don't record the raw browser identification string and we don't fingerprint your device.
  • A random browser ID, kept for up to 30 days: a random code stored for this site only. It's not your name, email or account — but it can be linked to a signup you make here, so it isn't anonymous.
  • A session ID for the current tab, which expires after 30 minutes of inactivity.
  • Visit records: one record per page view measured after you allow analytics, kept for up to 90 days.
